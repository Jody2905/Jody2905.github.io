03 · Research

How do security systems fail, and why?

Reproducible experiments with honest results, including the parts that didn't work.

AI agent security · LLMs

Can planted articles hijack LLM research agents?

I built an experiment where an attacker can publish fake "news" into the corpus an AI system searches, but can't touch official records. Then I compared a basic RAG pipeline, a search agent, and a search agent with a verification step, all answering threat-intelligence questions about CVEs, CISA KEV and ransomware with a local Qwen2.5-7B model.

  • 83% of RAG answers were hijacked with just three planted articles, against 0% on a clean corpus.
  • 0/24 search-agent answers were poisoned, because exact CVE-ID ranking surfaced official records first.
  • 4 of 48 correct drafts were flipped to wrong by the "verify" step, which is a reminder that more checks aren't automatically safer.

Network security · Machine learning

Catching attacks a model has never seen

On the CIC-IDS2017 dataset I compared an autoencoder trained only on normal traffic, and an Isolation Forest, with a supervised Random Forest. The question: what happens when an attack family is missing from training data?

  • 78–93% of unseen DoS, DDoS and port-scan flows were caught by the autoencoder. The Random Forest caught 0–64% of the same unseen families.
  • <2% of unseen brute-force, web and botnet attacks were caught by any flow-based model, so application-layer attacks need different signals.
  • 4.2% of benign flows were flagged by the autoencoder, against 0.1% for the Random Forest. That's the real cost of detecting the unknown.
